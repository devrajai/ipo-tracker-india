How to Pick an IPO — The Full Checklist

A vetted selection strategy, step by step

1. Read the RHP first — always

Open the Red Herring Prospectus (use the RHP/DRHP button on any IPO). Skip to: objects of the issue (where is the money going — repayment of promoter loans is a warning sign), promoter holding before/after, and the risk factors. Ten minutes here beats a hundred opinions.

2. Judge the business, not the buzz

Look for revenue growth over 3 years, stable or expanding margins, and a moat — customer concentration above ~25% from one client is a genuine risk in many SME IPOs. Use the Glossary ratios: ROE above ~15% sustained, ROCE comfortably above the company's borrowing cost, D/E below ~1 (higher is acceptable only in capital-intensive sectors).

3. Value it against listed peers

Find 2–3 listed companies in the same business and compare P/E and P/B at the issue price. If the IPO is priced at a big premium to profitable listed peers without faster growth, the listing pop is already priced in. Loss-making companies: compare EV/Sales instead.

4. Read subscription quality, not just quantity

  • QIB anchor book filled on day one — institutions did their homework and committed early. Strong signal.
  • QIB filling only on the last day — institutions are hedging on retail frenzy, not conviction. Weak signal.
  • Massive RII subscription with thin QIB — hype-driven; worst combination for listing-day risk.

5. Use GMP as a thermometer, not a forecast

GMP tells you the mood in the grey market today. It is useful for sizing listing-day expectations when combined with subscription numbers, but it tells you nothing about the business. High GMP + weak fundamentals = a trading opportunity, not an investment. Low/zero GMP + strong fundamentals can be the better long-term hold.

6. SME IPOs: extra caution

SME listings have thinner volumes, wider spreads and 5% circuit filters. Small float means both violent pops and violent dumps. Cut position sizes, and never chase an SME listing above the circuit for the first hour.

7. Decide your exit before the listing day

Write it down before 9:45 AM: "I sell at open", "I sell if it hits X% gain", or "I hold for the long term because I studied the business". Deciding in the heat of a ticker is how listing gains turn into losses. Avoid anchoring to GMP — the market on listing day does not care what the grey market quoted.

8. Size the position for a total loss

Any single IPO application should be money you can lose entirely without changing your plans. IPO allocation is lottery-weighted for retail; never borrow to apply.
